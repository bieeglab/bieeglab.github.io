Biography

Daniel Mathalon is a psychiatrist and clinical neuroscientist who co-directs the Brain Imaging and EEG Laboratory with Judith Ford. He studies the brain mechanisms underlying schizophrenia and psychosis, from the clinical high-risk period through established illness. His clinical work includes caring for people who have recently developed psychosis or show early warning signs.

His research combines magnetic resonance imaging, electroencephalography, and event-related potentials to investigate brain structure, the timing of neural responses, and the organization of sensory and cognitive processing. A central goal is to improve early identification and intervention and to better understand which changes in brain function predict clinical outcomes.

Mathalon earned his BA at UC Berkeley and his PhD in Clinical Psychology at Indiana University. He completed medical school, psychiatry residency, and psychophysiology fellowship training at Stanford. His early neuroimaging work with Adolf Pfefferbaum examined how aging and head size influence brain measurements.

After research work at SRI International, he joined Yale’s faculty in 2000 and established a clinical and cognitive neuroscience laboratory. In 2007, he and Ford moved their collaboration to San Francisco and established BIEEGL at UCSF. Alongside research, he has taught medical students and psychiatry residents and mentored clinical neuroscience trainees.
