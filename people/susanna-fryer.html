Biography

Susanna Fryer is a clinical psychologist and Associate Professor in the UCSF Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences. Her research connects brain function, cognition, and behavior, with particular interests in motivation, reward processing, self-regulation, and vulnerability to mental illness.

Using neuroimaging, EEG, and neuropsychological methods, Fryer studies the cognitive features and brain differences associated with risk for psychopathology and the mechanisms through which behavioral interventions may help. Her research spans schizophrenia, depression, and related questions about emotional and attentional regulation.

Fryer completed her undergraduate work in Human Biology at Stanford, where an honors research experience sparked her interest in clinical neuroscience. She earned her PhD through the SDSU/UC San Diego Joint Doctoral Program in Clinical Psychology, specializing in neuropsychology, and completed her clinical internship at the San Francisco VA Medical Center.

Her clinical background includes cognitive-behavioral therapy and mindfulness-based approaches. She has supervised psychology trainees and provided these treatments in the San Francisco VA’s general psychiatry service. Her research has been supported by the NIH and the VA.

Outside the lab, she enjoys yoga, cooking, and crafting.
