Biography

Carlos Daniel Carrasco is a cognitive and computational neuroscientist studying attention, working memory, and decision-making with human EEG, fMRI, behavioral experiments, and multivariate methods. He investigates how the brain prioritizes rare and behaviorally relevant information and how neural signals may help identify risk for psychosis.

He earned his PhD in Neuroscience at UC Davis in 2024, working with Steven J. Luck at the Center for Mind and Brain. His research examined enhanced working-memory representations for rare events and showed how multivariate decoding can increase effect sizes across commonly studied ERP components.

At UCSF and the San Francisco VA, he works with Daniel Mathalon on large-scale EEG and fMRI data from AMP SCZ and NAPLS. His postdoctoral work includes P300 habituation, alpha event-related desynchronization, and PCA-based methods for isolating ERP signals. A recurring theme is improving the rigor, reproducibility, and clinical usefulness of human neuroscience data.

Outside research, he enjoys exploring the outdoors, playing guitar, and spending time with friends.
