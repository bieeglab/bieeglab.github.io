Biography

Judith Ford co-directs the Brain Imaging and EEG Laboratory with Daniel Mathalon. Her research focuses on psychosis, especially auditory verbal hallucinations, and on how the brain distinguishes sensations generated by our own actions and thoughts from events in the outside world.

Ford uses neurophysiological methods to test whether difficulties predicting self-generated sensations contribute to hallucinations. When a sensation is not correctly predicted, it may be experienced as coming from an external source. Her work investigates this possibility by recording auditory-cortex responses to self-generated sounds.

This research examines sensory prediction errors and the efference-copy and corollary-discharge systems that help the brain anticipate the consequences of action. Alongside studies of vocalization, Ford has used EEG and fMRI during tasks in which people press a button to produce a sound or visual stimulus. These paradigms connect human studies of predictive coding with questions that can also be investigated in animal models.

Ford earned her BA in Psychology and her PhD in Neuroscience at Stanford, with dissertation guidance from Steven A. Hillyard at UC San Diego. After an NIH postdoctoral fellowship through UCSF, she returned to Stanford and later joined its psychiatry faculty. She moved to Yale in 2004 and established her UCSF laboratory in 2007.

Her research portfolio also includes studies of cognition after COVID-19, auditory steady-state responses, agency, and the effects of a very-low-carbohydrate ketogenic diet on neural network function in schizophrenia.
