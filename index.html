Many perspectives.
One complex brain.

A clinical and cognitive neuroscience laboratory dedicated to understanding schizophrenia.

At the Brain Imaging and EEG Laboratory (BIEEGL), we study brain structure and function to better understand the mechanisms underlying psychotic symptoms. Our work brings together brain imaging, electroencephalography (EEG), and the study of cognition.

Comparing brain activity in people with schizophrenia and people without the condition helps us investigate how differences in brain function relate to experience. Our research also asks how the brain predicts and responds to sensations we generate ourselves, including speech.

Our lab is affiliated with the University of California, San Francisco and the San Francisco VA.

fMRI / STRUCTURE & FUNCTION

BRAIN IMAGING · fMRI

Seeing the bigger picture

The lab uses brain imaging to compare brain structure and function in people with schizophrenia and comparison participants. Functional MRI (fMRI) maps changes in blood oxygenation associated with brain activity, helping us investigate how brain systems support cognition and mental health.

EEG / NEURAL ACTIVITY

ELECTROPHYSIOLOGY

Listening to brain signals

EEG captures the timing of brain activity. Our research examines responses to sounds and auditory oddball tasks, as well as brain signals during decision-making and gambling tasks. Simultaneous EEG and fMRI recordings connect the timing of electrical activity with patterns of brain function.

COGNITION / MENTAL HEALTH

CLINICAL NEUROSCIENCE

Connecting brain and experience

As a clinical and cognitive neuroscience lab, we combine brain-based measures with cognitive tasks, clinical assessments, and reports of lived experience. Together, these approaches help us investigate how differences in brain function relate to perception, thinking, and psychotic symptoms.

UCSF PATH PROGRAM

A path toward recovery.

The UCSF Path Program supports young people experiencing early psychosis or signs of increased risk, working with participants and families to build an individualized approach to care.

Its services include assessment, therapy, medication management, family education, and support with school and work. The program also studies early indicators of psychosis and approaches to treatment. Daniel Mathalon leads its research team as principal investigator.

AMP SCZBIOMARKERS / RISK / DISCOVERY

ACCELERATING MEDICINES PARTNERSHIP

Advancing the science of risk.

The Accelerating Medicines Partnership Schizophrenia (AMP SCZ) program is an international research effort developing tools to identify early risk for schizophrenia and better predict progression to psychosis and other outcomes.

The program brings together public and private partners to improve understanding of biological pathways and identify targets for early treatment. Its official website shares study information, scientific protocols, data access guidance, and research resources.
